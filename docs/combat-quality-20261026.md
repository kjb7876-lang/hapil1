# Existing combat quality: camera, visible shots and sustained pressure

Baseline: `346bee26788b789168fca50f63f12a09f6869c2d`. Runtime source: `59b999d8daf276d36ea38ea19864d734a7eea10b`. This is an update to the existing landscape game. It adds no campaign, external Persona turn system, original image/audio asset, or save format.

## Concrete problems and changes

- RC153 discarded accumulated encounter bounds when the layout changed by one pixel. RC223 mixed layout dimensions, visual viewport dimensions and a second camera anchor, then used a discontinuous small-screen zoom threshold. The camera now retains encounter visual profiles, refits them only for actual view changes, shares the fitted anchor and uses continuous optional view scaling. Optional zoom-out cannot reduce the final map footprint below 90% when full bodies and real safe insets can fit. An impossible viewport keeps body/safe-area correctness and reports the lower coverage. No artificial passing percentage is claimed for it.
- A failed minimum-body budget previously fell back to full size. It now uses the existing minimum uniform 0.5 actor scale and reduces only the mathematically necessary decorative gutter. Body, torso and bitmap-contact measurement use the same native scale. Actual safe insets are retained.
- Native world shake, attached-overlay shake, late enemy-impact shake and RC128's copy-and-shift of the whole canvas could move the screen even when the camera numbers were stable. Combat displacement is now zero. The native cue is still consumed, and accepted hit flashes, sound, bounded feedback cues, settings values and noncombat effects remain. The camera settings subscribe to effective percent changes and release their listener when closed.
- Legacy ordinary/midboss/boss shots pass through a different painter and opacity path from Persona. The final readability wrapper retains the exact source alpha, image dimensions, position, matrix and unmodified contact-probe paint. It strengthens contrast within that alpha and the nonzero body-opacity floor; explicit zero opacity, warnings, fading ghosts, allies, persistent fields and Persona's own nine-skill presentation keep their rules. The existing visibility predicate suppresses already removed packets. The first cold frame paints the original immediately; idle work prepares bounded cached variants. The cache limit is 8 MiB.
- RC95 applied Story's factor to the complete warning/stagger deadline and selected one lead owner for a long phase. Long signature-cast reuse also blocked unrelated pressure. A private pressure channel now schedules from the actual final release plus role recovery, scales recovery alone, and selects ready owners fairly. Existing salvos fill only available gaps around native major warning/active windows. Whole-volley admission, rollback, native 56/40 supplemental-admission caps, committed packets and unique signature reuse remain. Existing echo producers have their own limits and can make the total raw queue larger; the browser records that total separately from actual admission decisions. Dedicated Cosmic arsenals that wait for their own queue to drain retain their independent producer and are not kept permanently busy by supplemental shots.

## CPU evidence and its limits

`tests/rc231-camera-stability-unit.cjs` executes the production camera/layout and bounded native wrappers: fixed-view transforms remain exact; actual one-pixel layout steps have less than 1% raw final CSS-scale drift (maximum observed about 0.397%); visual-viewport-only oscillation does not change the final transform. Feasible short-view/safe-area fixtures preserve the final 90% map floor, including the intermediate-height fallback. These are synthetic decoded-envelope/viewport fixtures, not device footage.

`tests/rc233-screen-stability-unit.cjs` executes native shake functions, final wrappers, late feedback installation, the actual RC128 painter and settings component. Seven groups check real cue consumption, zero combat displacement, no whole-canvas recopy, retained sound/flash, unchanged HP/time/settings and subscription cleanup.

`tests/rc232-projectile-readability-unit.cjs` uses pinned Skia raster execution of the actual native RC13/RC71 painter. Its 177 checks include exact original alpha, destination/transform and contact-probe replay, default-opacity nonregression, explicit zero/terminal/warning protections, immediate cold fallback, idle preparation and warm-cache allocation/pixel-work bounds. The `.2` quiet-opacity fixtures gain about 4.97–5.31 times the RGB contrast signal; these values do not substitute for visible native browser frames.

`tests/rc231-pressure-cadence-unit.cjs` compares the old/new two scheduling policies with the same extracted native producer, warning, motion and retirement dependencies. In a ten-second CPU clock fixture, Story boss salvo announcements increase from 3 to 5; the gap from the last actual release to the next announcement falls from 2.035 to about 0.702 seconds. The final staggered packet of a salvo may release after its announcement window; the count is not a claim that every packet completed within ten seconds. Mobile Story announces 3 to 4 salvos with a peak 38/40 packets; an admission-cap wait is retained. Midboss announcements increase from 5 to 7. The first native salvo's physical and warning fields stay equal; the new transient pressure marker is separately disclosed. Dream's authored boss salvo behavior remains. The actual save serializer does not persist projectile queues or private pressure deadlines.

`tests/rc231-native-admission-domain-unit.cjs` executes the unchanged native echo producer and final guarded emitter. Its 35 checks show why raw queue length is not a universal admission cap: native echoes can add three packets at 56/40, retain their own limit of 12, and preserve prior packets. The guarded producer rejects new packets at those raw totals and fills exactly two available slots with its primary/density pair. Browser receipts separately require the actual guarded decisions, rejected-emission silence, pressure-packet provenance and echo-family bound; raw queue peaks remain visible in the report.

## Browser and release evidence

`tests/rc233-quality-browser.cjs` serves immutable baseline Git blobs and the candidate from separate paths on the same localhost server. It uses the prescribed Chrome Stable sandbox, actual native actors, producers, RAF, source images, floor painter and final camera. It records the exact tested Git SHA and served bundle hashes. Initial ready clocks, full authored enemy HP, canonical solo host, player protection and paused paint probes are explicit fixtures; clocks and casts are not reset during live multi-cycle observations. Incomplete baseline controls are recorded as incomplete rather than forced to pass or used to claim improvement.

Both releases receive the same 60-second observation bound per fixture. The prior candidate run stopped at an incorrect raw-global-queue cap assertion, and its 30-second baseline controls sometimes ended before three complete cycles; those logs do not establish a candidate camera pass or a complete cadence comparison. The updated tests retain three completed generic cycles, the separate major-pattern checks, all raster/camera gates and the 15% Story-boss cadence criterion. Existing RC126/RC127 repeat-volley fixtures now wait for the actual final-release/recovery deadline and test rejection immediately before it; Story recovery still receives its two-times policy factor.

Camera drift, raw one-pixel resize, actual native map matrices, release/flight pixel contrast, source-alpha/geometry preservation, warm-cache work, actual complete salvos, owner fairness and native major completion are separate measurements. Five actual advancing battle frames are distinct from five paired isolated warning/release/flight review sheets. All original full-size images and receipts accompany the CI artifact.

The existing CPU, mode/save/input, rotation, integration, camera/finale and narration gates remain required at the final checkout SHA. Extension 159 appends the exact frozen runtime source while retaining the first 158 manifests. Results and deployment identifiers belong to the PR and CI run receipts, not a claim that the CPU fixtures completed all campaigns naturally. Headless/software rendering and mobile emulation do not verify physical devices or hardware GPU performance.
